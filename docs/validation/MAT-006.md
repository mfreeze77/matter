## Grouping and provenance behavior

Adapter execution/event keys identify happenings independently of observation
and continuing-matter identity. Scope, namespace, and exact value are framed
structurally; there is no wording, source-digest, timestamp, or run-based
fallback. The immutable grouping policy binds allowed event-key and group
namespaces and the versioned set/lineage behavior. Memberships are many-to-many,
can be empty, and are stored as canonically ordered sets. Duplicated command
members are refused, while mere reordering produces an unchanged result.

An existing execution key cannot silently attach another report or alter event
details. Explicit replacements retain occurrence identity, kind, keys, time,
original provenance, and creation receipt. Empty historical shells remain
readable but do not contribute to counts over selected observations. Exact
retries retain the original operation result after later corrections and restart.

Root evidence may have one declared provenance group or an explicit unknown
reason. Parented reports inherit the union of their observation parents' groups,
regardless of a misleading source-origin label. A summary of groups A and B
retains both groups; it neither creates a third nor collapses them into one.
Shared ancestors are read once per resolution and watched at every traversed
observation identity. Unassigned roots, unknown roots, evaluator-only roots,
missing ancestors, and non-observation parents remain explicit unresolved
entries. Cross-scope lineage and incompatible pins are refused; the traversal
budget bounds referenced lineage nodes, including missing and unsupported
parents.

Count queries use one consistent snapshot over an explicit observation cohort.
They count the selected observations and their current direct occurrence
memberships, and resolve provenance for that cohort. Unselected peers sharing an
occurrence do not inflate the selected cohort's provenance groups. Loaded
ancestors do not inflate observation counts. Source-selection coverage is
required and preserved separately from provenance-resolution coverage. Tests
cover complete, partial, failed, not-yet-expected, unpublished, and manual-review
source coverage, including empty selections. Zero with incomplete coverage is
never asserted as established absence.

## Concurrency, recovery, and integrity

Preparation captures one snapshot and preserves caller-supplied pins. Execution
checks current revisions and repeats ancestry-watch queries under the same
write transaction. The following races passed:

- A root correction is prepared while no descendant occurrence is registered.
  Another command creates an occurrence containing a transitive summary. The
  frozen correction receives a durable `E_REVISION_CONFLICT`; a new command
  includes and correctly revisions that dependent occurrence.
- A membership command is prepared under one root assignment. A later root
  correction commits first. The stale membership cannot publish the old groups
  and leaves no occurrence or index behind.
- Two real spawned processes compete to replace one membership revision. Only
  one commits; the losing command retains its durable refusal and a fresh
  decision can append the next revision.
- Two real spawned processes compete to create occurrences under one execution
  key. Exactly one occurrence is created, with no orphan losing occurrence.

The interruption suite calls `os._exit` in spawned processes, bypassing Python
cleanup, during a batch that splits membership and corrects a root assignment.
Death before receipt or before commit leaves the old occurrence, old assignment,
old membership index, and counts intact, with no new occurrence, execution-key
binding, membership index, or journal entry. Retrying the same command can then
commit the entire batch. Death after commit recovers both sides of the split,
the assignment revision, indexes, common receipt, and original result without
running the handler again.

An injected acknowledgement failure after commit yields
`E_STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE`; exact retry recovers the complete committed result.
Restart and verified backup/new-file restore preserve grouping counts,
membership and assignment histories, direct/ancestry watches, exact key lookup,
and command replay. Observation payloads retain their separate backup boundary.

Independent review found a schema-reference typo and an error-path issue:
`StorageError` inherits `ValueError`, so a broad structural catch around a
watched port read could convert a transient outage or stale read into an
evidence refusal. The final code separates port reads from structural catches
and preserves unavailable-schema failures. Regressions prove that an outage
creates no false terminal journal and the exact command succeeds after recovery;
a missing expected pin remains a durable revision conflict. Stored projection
shape, actual record kind, scope, schema digest, lineage digest, and ancestry
watches are checked before they are used. No material review finding remains.
